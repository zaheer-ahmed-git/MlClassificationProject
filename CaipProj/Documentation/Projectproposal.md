# Machine Learning–Based Maintenance Cost Prediction for WAPDA Staff Colonies

This project proposes the development of a proof-of-concept application that uses **machine learning to estimate the maintenance cost for a given WAPDA house or residential apartment during the next twelve months** .

WAPDA manages a large number of residential houses and apartments across staff colonies in Pakistan. These properties require regular maintenance involving civil works, plumbing, electrical systems, roofing, painting, sewerage, and emergency repairs. At present, maintenance budgets are often prepared using historical averages, manual inspections, available complaints, and engineering judgment. While these methods are useful, they may not fully account for differences in property age, size, condition, occupancy, previous repair history, local environmental conditions, and changing labour and material prices.

Maintenance requirements vary significantly from one residential property to another. An older house with outdated plumbing, repeated complaints, roof leakage, or no recent renovation may require substantially more expenditure than a newer or recently repaired property. Without a data-driven forecasting system, WAPDA may face inaccurate budget estimates, delayed preventive maintenance, unexpected emergency expenditures, and difficulty prioritizing high-risk properties. The key challenge is therefore to develop a reliable mechanism that can analyse historical maintenance records and property-related factors to predict future maintenance costs at the individual house or apartment level.

The project will begin with a data-availability and quality assessment in coordination with the relevant WAPDA departments. Required information will include anonymized property identifiers, construction year, property type, covered area, occupancy status, renovation history, inspection or condition scores, maintenance complaints, completed work orders, repair categories, labour costs, material costs, contractor charges, and total actual expenditure. Publicly available inflation, construction material price, labour rate, and weather data may also be incorporated where relevant.

**Current data-strategy update (post-assessment):** WAPDA property-linked operational extracts are not currently obtainable under organizational privacy and authorization rules. The WASC files remain private seed evidence for the 101-unit residential framing inventory and descriptive analysis. Model training will use a harmonized corpus built from multiple real public datasets selected and mapped to this project’s schema (>500 properties), with explicit lineage and without presenting those labels as observed WAPDA outcomes. Details are in `DatasetPolicy.md` and `CodexFindings.md`.

The collected data will be cleaned, standardized, and organized into property-level historical records. Each record will represent the information available for a house or apartment during a defined period, along with the actual maintenance cost incurred during the following twelve months.

Several forecasting approaches will then be developed and compared, including historical cost baselines, linear regression, Random Forest, and gradient-boosting models. The performance will be evaulated using metrics such as **Mean Absolute Error, Root Mean Squared Error, and their ability to correctly identify high-maintenance-cost properties for better decision support** . It shall also be evaluated if the peformance evaluation could be done across multiple property types, age groups and cost ranges from different colonies to ensure the reliability.

The best-performing model can be integrated into a web-based proof-of-concept application. The users shall be able to select or enter a property, review its relevant information, and receive an estimated twelve-month maintenance cost. For an extended version, we may also consider the risk category, a prediction range, and the main factors influencing the estimate. The application may also provide colony-level summaries, high-risk property lists, estimated budget requirements, and comparisons between predicted and actual costs.


The application will help WAPDA prepare more accurate maintenance budgets and prioritize properties that are likely to require costly or urgent repairs. It shall have direct relevance to preventive infrastructure maintenance, public-sector budgeting and procurement planning. It will also support reduce unexpected expenditure, and provide decision-makers with transparent, data-driven estimates for better allocation of maintenance resources. We aim to develop a decision support tool rather than replacing cost allocation judgement. Moreover, we aim to provide explanations to improve accountability as why particular properties are classified as high risk and how to determine the maintenance priorities to provide better opportunities to improve maintenance policies and procurement practices.

The proposed implementation will follow four classical stages: data assessment and preparation, model development and validation, application development, and pilot testing using selected WAPDA staff colonies.
